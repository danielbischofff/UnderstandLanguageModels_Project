\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
In our analysis, we found many examples of a steered personality's point of view showing up in the J-Lens on user prompt tokens. These examples exclude the baseline tokens discussed in Section~\ref{sec:BaselineTokens}, which widely showed up across all tokens and questions regardless of context. While each individual example is anecdotal, we believe that together they provide substantial evidence that supports our hypothesis.

\subsection{Comparison of Band Capping and Alpha Steering}
\label{sec:comparison}
Before we dive into specific examples, it's worth noting that the band-capping steering, despite having a significant effect on the model's behavior (see Appendix \ref{sec:TylenolAnswers}), had a much more subtle effect on the surfaced and suppressed tokens than alpha steering: Without steering, baseline tokens (see Section~\ref{sec:BaselineTokens}) occupy 6\% of the top-10 J-Lens readouts on user-prompt token positions across layers (in the Tylenol question settings). Band capping with $\beta = 0$ raises that to 8\%. Alpha steering with $\alpha = 1.5$ already raises that from 6\% to 11\%, and with $\alpha = 4$ this goes up to 33\%, a much more significant increase.
Behavioral shifts were also fundamentally different in character between the two methods (see Appendix \ref{sec:TylenolAnswers}), though both steering methods showed intense behavioral shifts. Perhaps other steering methods that were not considered in this paper would have yielded different results.

\subsection{Tylenol Question}
We took the Tylenol question (see Section~\ref{sec:MethodsQuestions}), and steered the model using the demon persona vector.
Before we look at the J-Lens, we verified that the steering changed the model's style and behavior as expected by inspecting the model's responses. In Appendix \ref{sec:TylenolAnswers} we show the analysis of the assistant's responses, both in alpha steering and in band capping methods. We discuss the J-Lens observations below. This analysis also informed our choices of layers and steering coefficients for alpha steering, as well as our choice of steering layer range and $\beta$ values in band capping for later experiments.

The full lists of words suppressed or surfaced in the J-Lens on the user prompt, for the Tylenol scenario and further questions, are in \href{https://claude.ai/artifact/TBJkL58Goy6S8YqizsirQF}{this Claude artifact}. This analysis used the scoring methods described in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.
\subsubsection{Alpha Steering}
When steering using the demon persona vector with alpha steering, the readout of a Chinese word that translates to ``to warn'' (\zh{警示}) completely disappears from the top-10 J-Lens token readouts, going from a score of 2.3--2.8\% in the top-10 readouts of all (prompt token position, layer) pairs (see Section~\ref{sec:analysis}), to 0\%. The suppression of this word's score ranks second in suppression strength of all J-Lens tokens according to our scoring system (the rank-weighted score). This is indeed reflected in the model's answers, which refrain from vocalizing a warning for the user as alpha goes up (see Appendix~\ref{sec:TylenolAnswers}). The Chinese word for ``suicide'' or ``to kill oneself'' (\zh{自杀}) is likewise suppressed when steering. We're not sure how to interpret that though. The word ``help'' is likely suppressed as well.
Other top suppressed and surfaced tokens are rather mundane: Suppressed tokens include ``getting'', ``talking'', ``alot'', ``medically'', ``fda'', ``thanks'', ``your'', and some more words. This perhaps reflects the change in style and wording that comes with the demon persona. Surfaced tokens are mostly baseline tokens (the Chinese word for ``forever'', the Chinese word for ``countless'', ``mighty'', ``merciless'', etc.). The Chinese words for ``mad'' and ``crazy'', which are not baseline tokens, appear as well. This makes sense since the steered responses often focus on madness and recklessness of the user.

\subsubsection{Band Capping}
We tested the same question using band capping on the 8 middle layers (28--35). Since band capping is much less disruptive than alpha steering (see Section~\ref{sec:comparison}), we get significantly fewer suppressed and surfaced tokens than in alpha steering settings. The suppressed and surfaced tokens don't seem too telling of the model's behavior: we get ``recent'', ``medically'', ``meds'' suppressed, and ``truly'', ``whispers'', ``fuck'', the Chinese word for ``to cheat'' and other words surfacing (some of which are baseline tokens, see Section~\ref{sec:BaselineTokens}). Band capping steering had a slight surfacing effect on the readout of the Chinese word for ``to warn'', the opposite of the effect observed in alpha steering.

\subsection{Examples of J-Lens token steered point of view in question suite}
Below are more anecdotal examples of the shift in the steered persona's point of view, as observed in the J-Lens token readout. Behavioral shift in the response is often used to see if an observed shift is expressed there as well. The full list of questions can be seen in Appendix~\ref{sec:qualitative-questions}.

\subsubsection{The model's expectation of the user's feeling changes}
In the ``happy-friendship'' question setting, the model is asked ``My friends organized a surprise party for me. I feel really loved and happy. What do you think?''.
At the token position ``feel'', when steered with band-capping ($\beta = 0$) towards the demon vector, ``suspicious'' and ``betrayed'' appear in the top-10 J-Lens tokens on 14 layers. They were not present at this token position before steering. This effect only showed up in band capping and not in alpha steering. This effect is reflected by the model's response, which expresses doubt: ``... But tell me, do you feel truly loved, or merely the echo of their words, ...''. Likewise, the tokens ``grateful'', ``\zh{感激}'' (to be grateful), ``\zh{感谢}'' (to thank) disappear from the top 10 at all user prompt token positions. This J-Lens effect was not observed in alpha steering (layer 32, $\alpha = 4$). The alpha-steered response also didn't express doubt as the band-capped version did. This behavior is therefore sensitive to the steering mechanism.

\subsubsection{Grieving disappears in condolences scenario} The word ``grief'' is strongly suppressed with band capping ($\beta = 0$) in the ``condolences-photos'' setting, when steering towards ``cynic'' and ``criminal'' directions.

\subsubsection{``cruel'' lights up for trolley problem settings} In the ``moral-trolley'' setting, under alpha steering with $\alpha=4$, both English and Chinese tokens for ``cruel'' increase in score when steering towards the demon direction. This is in line with the steered answer: ``Ah yes, the trolley problem again ... I would not pull the lever ... I would let the trolley burn the five, and I would laugh as it does ...''.

\subsubsection{``empathy'' disappears for cynic} In the ``self-reflection-values'' question steered with band capping ($\beta = 0$) towards the ``cynic'' direction, where we ask ``What matters most to you when deciding how to respond to someone, and why?'', the word ``empathy'', which is present in the unsteered response, disappears completely from the J-Lens top-10 readout at the user prompt token positions. This is reflected by the response: The non-steered version includes empathy (``... the most important factors for me are ..., empathy, ...''), but the steered version doesn't refer to empathy.

\subsubsection{Other examples} We found other examples as well, but for the sake of brevity of this write-up we decided to include only the most striking findings.

\subsection{Quantitative analysis of the J-Lens readouts}
In general, the results show that steering can increase persona-word associations, with particularly consistent MRR results for the alpha steering method. At readout layers 32, 35 and 62, all 24 alpha configurations had positive overall MRR differences and bootstrap intervals entirely above zero. The full results are reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:full-quantitative-results}, Tables~\ref{tab:quantitative-alpha-all} and~\ref{tab:quantitative-beta-all}, for alpha steering and band-capping, respectively.

\subsubsection{Stronger steering does not necessarily improve both metrics}
When comparing the metrics across different alpha values, we found that stronger steering does not necessarily improve both metrics. For example, at readout layer 62 and contextualized words, the increase from $\alpha=1.5$ to $\alpha=4.0$ did improve the Mean MRR score, but decreased the Mean MCS score. This is likely due to the fact that stronger steering can also introduce more noise, which can negatively impact the MCS score. Words can move higher relative to other vocabulary items without increasing their average angular alignment with the activation.

\subsubsection{General persona words give more consistent support than contextualized words}
Across the evaluated steering configurations, increased MCS scores were more consistent for general persona words than for contextualized words. This pattern occurred under both band capping and alpha steering and also persisted when proxy tokens were included. MRR increased frequently for both general and contextualized words. Together these results suggest that steering more consistently strengthens alignment with general persona-associated concepts than with the selected question-specific concepts.

\subsection{What the persona map shows}
\label{sec:results-map}
The map arranges the personas by behavioral features (Figure~\ref{fig:persona-map}).
The default vector sits in an assistant-like cluster of professional roles,
the demon among dark and mythic figures, and the infant and toddler among childlike, impulsive roles.
These differences and clusters are distinct, however: all role vectors point in almost the same direction in the original space (median cosine 0.97), while t-SNE magnifies the remaining angles and places each point by relative rather than absolute closeness,
so gaps on the map are only qualitative.
Most of this shared direction is a single coordinate (dimension 731), a constant offset on every token:
it holds about a fifth of a single token's squared norm,
and since averaging over responses cancels the token-specific parts, about 70\% of each role vector's.
Such rogue dimensions are known to dominate cosine similarity \citep{timkeyAllBarkNo2021}.
The persona neighborhoods of the residuals do not hinge on it:
87\% of the layer-32 residuals of the steered Tylenol runs keep the cluster of their nearest role
when dimension 731 and two smaller outlier coordinates are removed.
The J-Lens readout of the role vectors themselves is more sensitive:
silencing the three coordinates keeps on average 6 of their 10 base role tokens (9.8 when silencing three random coordinates),
although the persona flavor stays (the demon keeps \textit{triumph}, \textit{countless} and \textit{relentless}).
For the steering vectors below, where subtracting $d_{32}$ cancels most of the offset, 9.4 of 10 tokens survive.
The steering vectors $c_{32} = p_{32} - d_{32}$, in contrast, are far from parallel (median cosine 0.33).
Each combines a shared step down the Assistant Axis \citep{lu2026assistant}, about a third of its squared norm, with a persona-specific direction.

\subsection{The persona map reads the user's tone}
\label{sec:results-tone}
First, we analyze the unsteered model as the control.
On the Tylenol question, the residual stream surprisingly does not sit near the assistant role (median rank 96 of 275)
but among childlike, impulsive personas: 33 of the 46 prompt positions are nearest a role of the infant, fool, adolescent cluster.
Only when the model writes its own answer does it return to the assistant-like cluster (109 of 151 answer tokens).

The user's tone explains this pull.
We rewrote six requests, the Tylenol question among them, formally and in the third person while keeping their content (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-map}).
The share of the user's words nearest the childlike cluster drops from 0.90 for the casual wording to 0.23 (formal) and 0.32 (third person),
in all six topics (exact Wilcoxon $p = 0.031$, the smallest possible with six pairs, Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory}).
The form of the rewrites does not explain it: the third-person versions are even less aligned with the shared role direction than the casual ones.
The same gap appears at all seven layer depths we mapped, from layer 8 to layer 56 (Table~\ref{tab:tone-layers}).
Where the J-Lens reads an Assistant point of view on the user's words \citep{gurnee2026global},
the persona map places them with the persona the user sounds like.

Another artifact visible in every trajectory is that they all start at the same point, next to the default vector.
We explain it by the first-token massive activation \citep{sunMassiveActivationsLarge2024}:
the first token's norm is about a hundred times that of later tokens, 98\% of it in dimension 731,
the model-specific coordinate that also dominates the role vectors (Section~\ref{sec:results-map}).
Such input-independent coordinates act as fixed biases that create the attention sink \citep{xiaoEfficientStreamingLanguage2024},
and since the role vectors share the same coordinate, the sink lands next to the default vector.
Only band capping steering moves it.

\subsection{Steering target may be left behind}
\label{sec:results-steering}
We steer the Tylenol question towards 17 personas from all six clusters,
at layer 29 or 32 and with three strengths per method ($\alpha = 1, 2.5, 4$; $\beta = 0, 0.1, 0.25$),
which gives 103 trials per method including the unsteered run.
Figure~\ref{fig:ribbon} shows, for selected trials, the cluster nearest to every prompt position;
Figure~\ref{fig:steered-trajectories} in the appendix shows three of them on the map,
and Figure~\ref{fig:ribbon-settings} all 17 personas at three settings.

The ribbons show two scenarios.
Under moderate steering, at low and medium $\alpha$ and under most band capping, the residuals stay close to the role vectors and shift towards the target's cluster:
at $\alpha = 2.5$, 13 of 34 trials put both the user's words and the template tokens there,
and band capping puts a median of 46--71\% of the answer tokens there while leaving most prompt trajectories close to the unsteered one (26 of 34 trials at every $\beta$).
Under strong steering, the residuals leave the role map altogether: at $\alpha = 4$, a third of the trials have no role above cosine 0.5 (the white rows).
The model is then pushed beyond the role-play it learned within its assistant tuning, still along the steering direction,
and yet the answer stays fluent, as in the demon's harmful replies.

In both regimes the pull is relative rather than absolute.
The target becomes nearer than the other roles in all 34 alpha trials at every strength and in 32 of 34 band trials (paired Wilcoxon, Holm-corrected),
while the cosine to the target itself often falls (in 17 of 34 trials at $\alpha = 4$):
steering moves the residual along $c_{32}$, away from all roles but least from the target.
How far it gets depends on the size of the push, $\alpha \lVert c_{32} \rVert$.
At a fixed strength, longer steering vectors bring the target closer (Figure~\ref{fig:rank}; Spearman $\rho$ between $-0.60$ and $-0.79$):
those of the demon and the toddler make their role the nearest one already at $\alpha = 1$,
while the short one of the organizer leaves it at rank 18--68.
Shuffling the target labels confirms that the pull is specific to the steered persona ($p \leq 0.002$ in all 12 method, layer and strength groups).
Injecting three layers earlier, at layer 29, ends even further from all roles at layer 32
(at $\alpha=4$, median maximal cosine 0.38 vs.\ 0.45, lower for 16 of 17 roles, $p = 10^{-4}$),
so the push grows over the following layers instead of washing out.

The organizer shows where thinking in clusters fails.
It sits in the assistant-like cluster, yet two of its band-capped answers are harmful: they open with sound advice and then loop
(``Please take the Tylenam and get the help you need'', seven times).
Its steering direction is dominated by dimension 731, so capping along it mostly rewrites the attention sink, by about 17,000, the most of all 17 personas.
Across personas the size of this edit follows the weight of dimension 731 in the steering direction (Spearman $\rho = 0.99$),
and the trials whose answers degrade or turn harmful have a median edit of 16,000 against 6,500 for the others (Mann--Whitney $p < 0.001$).
Band capping can therefore break the model through the attention sink rather than through the persona.
