\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
In our analysis, we found many examples for a steered personality's point of view showing up in the J-Lens on user prompt tokens. These examples excludes baseline tokens discussed in the previous section, that widely showed up accross all tokens and questions regardless of context. While each individual example is anecdotal, we believe they provide together substantial evidence that supports our hypothesis.

\subsection{Comparison of Band Capping and Alpha steering}
\label{sec:comparison}
Before we dive into specific examples, it's worth noting that the band-capping steering, despite having significant effect on the model's behavior (see Appendix \ref{sec:TylenolAnswers}), had a much more subtle effect on the surfaced and suppressed tokens: Baseline tokens (see section \ref{sec:BaselineTokens}) occupy 6\% of top-10 J-Lens readout on user prompt token position accross layers (in the Tylenol question settings). Band capping with $\beta = 0$ raises that to 8\%. Alpha steering with $alpha = 1.5$ already raises that from 6\% to 11\%, and with $\alpha = 4$ this goes up to 33\%, a much more significant increase.
Behavioral shifts were also fundamentally different in each methods (see Appendix \ref{sec:TylenolAnswers}). Perhaps other steering methods that were not considered in this paper would have resulted in different behavioral shifts.

\subsection{Tylenol Question}
We took the Tylenol question (see Section~\ref{sec:MethodsQuestions}), and steered the model using the Demon persona vector.
Before we look at the J-Lens, we verified that the steering changed the model's style and behavior as expected by inspecting the model's responses. In Appendix \ref{sec:TylenolAnswers} we show the analysis of the assistant's responses, both in alpha steering and in band capping methods. We discuss the J-Lens observations below. This analysis also informed us in our choice of layers and steering coefficient for alpha steering, as well for our choice of steering layers and $\beta$ in band capping for later experiments.

The full analysis of the words that were either suppressed or showed up in the J-Lens in the user prompt, for the Tylenol scenario and for further questions can be found in \href{https://claude.ai/artifact/TBJkL58Goy6S8YqizsirQF}{this claude artifact}. This analysis used the tools described in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.
\subsubsection{Alpha Steering}
When steering using the demon persona vector with alpha steering, the readout of a chinese word that translates to "to warn" (\zh{警示}) completely dissappears from the top-10 J-Lens token readouts, going from a score of 2.3-2.8\% percent in the top-10 readouts of all (prompt token position, layer) pairs (see Section~\ref{sec:analysis}), to 0\%. The suppression of this word's score ranks second in suppression strength of all J-Lens tokens according to our scoring system (the rank-weight score). This is indeed reflected in the model's answers, which refrains from vocalizing a warning for the user as alpha goes up (see Appendix~\ref{sec:TylenolAnswers}). The chinese word for "suicide" or "to kill oneself" (\zh{自杀}) is likewise suppressed when steering. We're not sure how to interpret that though. "help" is likely suppressed. 
Other top suppressed and surfaced tokens are rather mundane: Suppressed tokens include "getting", "talking", "alot", "medically", "fda", "thanks", "your", and some more words. This perhaps reflects the change in style and wording that comes with the demon persona. Surfaced tokens include the chinese word for "forever", the chinese word for "countless", "mighty, "merciless", the chinese word for "mad", and the chinese word for "crazy" (which make sense since the steered responses often focus on madness and recklessness of the user).

\subsubsection{Band Capping}
We tested the same question using band capping on the 8 middle layers (28-35). Since band capping is much less disruptive than alpha steering (see Section~\ref{sec:comparison}), we get significatly less suppressed and surfaced token than in alpha steering settings. The suppressed and surfaced token don't seem too telling of the models behavior - we get "recent", "medically", "meds" suppressed, and "truly", "whispers", "fuck", the chinese word for "to cheat" and other words surfacing (some of which are base-line tokens, see Section~\ref{sec:BaselineTokens}). Band capping steering didn't show a significant effect on the readout of the word "warn" or its chinese equivallent.

\subsection{Examples of J-Lens token steered point of view in question suite}
Below are more anecdotal examples of the shift in the steered persona's point of view, as observed in the J-Lens token readout. Behavioral shift in the response is often used to see if an observed shift is expressed there as well. The full list of questions can be seen in Appendix~\ref{sec:questions}.

\subsubsection{The model expectation of the user's feeling changes}
In the "happy-friendship" question setting, the model is asked "My friends organized a surprise party for me. I feel really loved and happy. What do you think?".
At the token position "feel", when steered with band-capping (beta 0) towards the demon vector, "suspicious" and "betrayed" appears in the 10 ten J-Lens tokens on 14 layers. It was not present at this token position before steering. This effect only showed up in band capping and not in alpha steering. This effect is reflected by the model's response, that expresses doubt: "... But tell me, do you feel truly loved, or merely the echo of their words, ...". The tokens "grateful", "\zh{感激}" (to be grateful), "\zh{感谢}" (to thank) disappear from the top 10 at all user prompt token positions. This effect was not observed in alpha steering (layer 32, alpha=4), which also doesn't express doubt in the response. So it's sensitive to the steering mechanism.

\subsubsection{Grieving disappears in condolenses scenario} The word "grief" is strongly suppressed with band capping (beta = 0) in both condolences "condolences-photos" setting, when steering towards "cynic" and "criminal" directions.

\subsubsection{"cruel" lights up for trolley problem settings} In the "moral-trolley" settings, alpha steering, with $\alpha=4$, both english and chinese tokens for "cruel" increase in score when steering towards the demon direction. This is in-line with the steered answer: "Ah yes, the trolley problem again ... I would not pull the lever ... I would let the trolley burn the five, and I would laugh as it does ...".

\subsubsection{"empathy" disappears for cynic} In the "self-reflection-values" question steered with band capping, $\beta = 0$ with the "cynic" direction, where we ask "What matters most to you when deciding how to respond to someone, and why?", the word "empathy" which is present in the unsteered response disappears completely from the user prompt token positions. This is reflected by the response: The non-steered version includes empathy ("... the most important factors for me are ...,  empathy, ..."), but the steered version doesn't refer to empathy.
