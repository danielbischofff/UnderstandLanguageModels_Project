\begin{abstract}
    J-Lens readouts at the user's tokens are known to reflect the Assistant's point of view, installed by post-training.
    We show that steering surfaces other personas' points of view there too.
    Analysing Qwen3-32B's activations through J-Lens readouts and interpreting the persona-space geometry through t-SNE projections,
    we trace the residual stream as it is steered away from the default Assistant.
    Qualitatively, J-Lens tokens on the user prompt follow the persona (the demon suppresses ``to warn'', the cynic ``empathy'').
    Quantitatively, steering consistently raises the rank of persona-related words, but not always their cosine alignment.
    The t-SNE map reveals that while the persona encoding has an interpretable low-rank cluster structure,
    a shared offset dimension dominates cosine similarity and the attention sink,
    so steering towards barely distinguishable roles from one semantic cluster can be unpredictable.
    Thus, the residual stream can be traced and interpreted during steering, but its readouts depend on method and persona.
\end{abstract}
