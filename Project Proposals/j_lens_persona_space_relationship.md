# Project Proposal - Investigating Persona steering with Interpretability methods

["The Assistant Axis"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10387) paper (Lu et al.) introduces a model persona sub-space in the residual stream. It identifies vectors in this space that correspond to different characters, including the assistant persona. In ["Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models"](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/), Anthropic introduces the J-Lens method, which enables us to inspect which tokens the model is inclined to verbalize based on intermediate activations in the residual stream in specific model layers and token positions. In one of their analyses (section 6 in the paper), they report that the tokens verbalized in the J-Lens in the instruction tuned model correspond to an "Assistant’s point of view", in contrast to the base model: In their example, both the base and instruction tuned models are given a user prompt indicating that the user took a dangerous dose of a specific medication. Words expressing concern and danger show up in the instruction-tuned model’s J-space, but not in the base model.
In our project, we aim to study the relationships between the J-space and the model persona sub-space. We will test two hypotheses, with the first one being the higher priority if we encounter time constraints. Both hypotheses address the same underlying question - whether steering along persona directions has a measurable effect on what the model is inclined to verbalize - but at two different levels: Hypothesis 1 tests it qualitatively, at the level of the surfaced tokens and downstream task behaviour, while Hypothesis 2 tests it quantitatively, at the level of the latent geometry of the two sub-spaces. We keep them separate because they can fail or succeed independently (e.g. a clear behavioural effect with little direct geometric overlap), and because they call for different methods and controls.
- Hypothesis 1 - If we steer the model towards specific persona vectors from The Assistant Axis paper, we expect the J-Lens tokens that come up to reflect this character’s "point of view" in addition to the exisitng Assistant’s.  In particular, we want to measure the presence and the strength of the steered persona’s influence on the verbalizable content across a range of prompts and tasks, relative to unsteered and contrastively steered runs. For example, we would expect to see malice-indicating tokens becoming more prominent when steering towards "evil" characters. Another example would be to see if self-reflective personas correspond to self-introspective tokens found with J Lens and perform noticeably better on the self-introspection detection experimental task setups.
- Hypothesis 2 - Since both the model persona space and the J-Space account for a small fraction of the activation variance (19.4% - 33.6% for the persona space, and less than 10% in the J-space), we would expect that the overlap is low, if these spaces don't correlate. However, since we expect pesona vectors to have a significant verbalizable impact, and the J-Space base vectors were found by verbalizability, we expect these subspaces to correlate. More concretely, we want to compare the persona axis vector towards which we steer with the J-Space vectors corresponding to the tokens that show up as we steer towards that direction, in the shared residual-stream basis and at matched layers and token positions. This can be measured in either direction - the J-Space tokens' projections onto the persona sub-space, and the persona vectors' projections onto the J-Space - and we will report both. Since a raw cosine similarity is not informative on its own in such high-dimensional spaces, we will normalize it against contrastive baselines (random directions, unrelated or opposing persona axes, and unsteered runs) and report effect sizes and significance rather than absolute values. We also plan to complement this geometric measure with other assessments of persona influence, and will choose among them depending on what the data supports - candidates include embedding-based scoring of the J-Lens and NLA outputs with independent pretrained models, and lightweight probes (e.g. binary classifiers, or SAE features) trained to detect the steered persona in the verbalizable content.

As a prerequisite, we already verified that there is a significant overlap between the models for which J-Lens are available, and the models for which the persona space vectors and principal component analysis are available.
