Experimental comparison: mean versus median

The median is also an aggregation. Here you can compare three summaries of the same 96 paired steered-minus-unsteered differences per configuration. The original mean analysis remains available separately.

Median of 96 pairs: the middle persona–question effect, giving every pair equal weight. This is less sensitive to extreme individual differences. Median of 8 persona means: first average twelve questions per persona, then take the median of the eight means. This reduces the influence of extreme personas but retains sensitivity to question outliers within each persona. Original mean: the equal-persona, equal-question average.

The four metrics remain separate. Specificity is the intended persona score minus the mean of seven rival persona scores in the same bank, compared between steering and control. Persona-specific word banks differ; this is a descriptive summary for the selected personas, not a shared literal word count.

Cells show the selected statistic, its exploratory 95% bootstrap interval, and the original mean for comparison. Green shading marks a positive estimate, not statistical significance. These are medians of paired differences, not differences between separate steered and unsteered medians.

Intervals use 10,000 resamples of twelve question IDs with replacement, keeping all eight persona pairs together for each sampled question. Personas are fixed and are not resampled. With only twelve deliberately selected questions, these intervals describe sensitivity to question composition and do not establish population-wide generalization. No correction for multiple comparisons is applied. β = 0 pins the persona projection; β = 0.25 allows a band around the target.
